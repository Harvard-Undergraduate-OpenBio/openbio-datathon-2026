# Track 2: Global Health — Overview

## Research Question

> Which interactions between patients, facilities, screening systems, treatment resources, and referral networks are failing in preeclampsia care, and which health-system intervention points appear most important?

## Focus

Teams analyze health-system factors contributing to preeclampsia morbidity across different country contexts. Using DHS Program data and related health-system resources, teams identify where the care continuum breaks down and propose context-appropriate interventions.

## Care Continuum

The preeclampsia care continuum includes:

1. **Antenatal care (ANC)** — Routine check-ups during pregnancy where blood pressure and proteinuria screening occur.
2. **Screening and detection** — Identification of preeclampsia risk through BP measurement, urine protein testing, and clinical assessment.
3. **Referral** — Transfer of high-risk patients to facilities capable of managing severe PE.
4. **Facility readiness** — Availability of trained staff, equipment, medications, and blood supply at referral facilities.
5. **Treatment** — Administration of magnesium sulfate (for seizure prevention), antihypertensives, and timely delivery.
6. **Transport** — Time and means of reaching a facility; a major delay in many settings.
7. **Postpartum care** — Monitoring and management of PE complications after delivery.

## Possible Outputs

- Identification of distinct health-system contexts (e.g., urban vs. rural, high vs. low facility density).
- Analysis of where delays occur in the care continuum (the "Three Delays" framework).
- Ranking of intervention points by potential impact.
- Context-specific intervention recommendations.
- Modeling of intervention effectiveness.
