Choose one focused angle, or adapt one to your team's interests. Go deep rather than wide, and be honest about what the data can and cannot support. The table shows where to start, not what to find. New to this? Questions 1, 2 and 7 each use a single dataset and fit the [workflow](3-workflow.md) directly.

| # | Question | Where to start |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which measured risk factors are most strongly associated with cardiovascular disease in women? | Dataset 1, logistic regression |
| 2 | How does blood pressure change with age in women, and how does that relate to CVD? | Dataset 1, fitted line |
| 3 | Do cholesterol and glucose levels add information beyond blood pressure and age? | Dataset 1, comparing models |
| 4 | Do lifestyle factors such as smoking or physical activity matter as much as clinical measures? | Dataset 1 or 2 |
| 5 | Do risk factors relate to heart attack risk differently for women and men? | Dataset 2 (synthetic, methods exercise) |
| 6 | How do income and stress relate to heart attack risk in a synthetic population? | Dataset 2 (synthetic, methods exercise) |
| 7 | Is a country's development level (SDI) related to its CVD incidence in women? | Dataset 3, Table S2 |
| 8 | Where did CVD incidence in women rise or fall most between 1990 and 2019? | Dataset 3, Table S2 |
| 9 | How has the mortality-to-incidence ratio changed across regions, and what might that say about care? | Dataset 3, Table S1 |
| 10 | How do individual risk factors connect to the unequal global burden of CVD in women? | Datasets 1 and 3, compared side by side |

## Before You Commit to a Question

- Dataset 2 is synthetic. Any finding from it is a methods exercise, not evidence about real people.
- Dataset 3 describes countries and regions. It cannot tell you about individual women.
- You cannot join Dataset 3 to Datasets 1 or 2 row by row. Question 10 compares patterns across scales, not people.
- Pick a question that gives you a regression graph. See [Step 4](3-workflow.md#4-build-a-model).
