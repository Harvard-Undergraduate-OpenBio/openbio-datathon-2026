# Glossary

[![Back](https://img.shields.io/badge/Back-Track_Overview-13deba?style=flat-square)](README.md)

Plain-language definitions, grouped by topic.

## Health Terms

- **Cardiovascular disease (CVD)** - Disease of the heart and blood vessels, including heart attack and stroke.
- **Heart attack** - Blocked blood flow to part of the heart muscle.
- **Blood pressure** - The force of blood on artery walls, written as two numbers such as 120/80 mmHg.
- **Systolic blood pressure** - The top number: pressure while the heart beats. `ap_hi` in Dataset 1.
- **Diastolic blood pressure** - The bottom number: pressure between beats. `ap_lo` in Dataset 1. It should always be lower than systolic.
- **Hypertension** - Persistently high blood pressure.
- **Cholesterol** - A fat-like substance in the blood. High levels are linked to CVD.
- **Glucose** - Blood sugar. High levels can signal diabetes.
- **Triglycerides** - A type of fat in the blood.
- **BMI (body mass index)** - Weight in kg divided by height in meters squared. A rough measure of body size.
- **Menopause** - The end of menstrual periods, usually around age 45 to 55, after which CVD risk in women rises.

## Population Health Terms

- **Global Burden of Disease (GBD)** - A large international study that estimates disease rates for every country.
- **Incidence** - New cases in a period. **Incident cases** is the count.
- **Mortality** - Deaths in a period.
- **ASIR (age-standardized incidence rate)** - New cases per 100,000 people, adjusted so populations with different age mixes can be compared fairly.
- **MIR (mortality-to-incidence ratio)** - Deaths divided by new cases. A rough signal of how deadly or poorly managed a disease is relative to how often it occurs.
- **SDI (socio-demographic index)** - A 0 to 1 score combining income, education and fertility. Higher means more developed.
- **EAPC (estimated annual percentage change)** - The average yearly change in a rate over a period, with a confidence interval.
- **Ecological fallacy** - The mistake of assuming a pattern seen across groups (such as countries) holds for individuals in them.

## Data Terms

- **Synthetic data** - Data generated by a computer to resemble real records. Useful for practice, not evidence about real people.
- **Outcome (target)** - The variable you are trying to explain, such as `cardio`.
- **Predictor (feature)** - A variable used to explain the outcome, such as age.
- **Binary variable** - A variable with two values, such as 0 and 1.
- **Ordinal variable** - Ordered categories, such as normal < above normal < well above normal. The order matters but the gaps are not equal.
- **Encoding** - Turning text values into numbers a model can use.
- **Plausibility rule** - A rule for which values are physically possible. Values outside it are treated as errors.
- **Cleaning log** - A record of each cleaning rule and how many records it affected.
- **Wide vs long format** - Wide has one column per year or measure. Long has one row per observation, which is easier to plot and model.

## Statistics Terms

- **Mean, median and standard deviation** - The average, the middle value and the typical spread around the average.
- **Correlation** - How strongly two variables move together. It does not show cause.
- **Confounder** - A variable linked to both the predictor and the outcome, which can create a misleading association. Age is a common one.
- **Linear regression** - Fits a straight line to predict a number from one or more predictors.
- **Slope** - How much the outcome changes per one-unit change in the predictor.
- **R² (R-squared)** - The share of variation in the outcome explained by the model, from 0 to 1.
- **Logistic regression** - Models the chance of a yes/no outcome from one or more predictors.
- **Odds ratio (OR)** - How the odds of the outcome change per unit of a predictor. 1 means no association.
- **Confidence interval (CI)** - A range of values consistent with the data. Wider means less certain.
- **p-value** - How surprising a result would be if there were no real effect. Smaller means more surprising.
- **Train/test split** - Fitting a model on part of the data and checking it on the rest, so it is judged on data it has not seen.
- **AUC** - How well a model ranks people with the outcome above those without. 0.5 is chance, 1 is perfect.
- **Residual** - The gap between an observed value and the model's prediction.
- **Forest plot** - A chart of estimates (such as odds ratios) with their confidence intervals, one row per predictor.
