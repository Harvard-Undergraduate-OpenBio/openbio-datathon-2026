# Responsible Use

[![Back](https://img.shields.io/badge/Back-Track_Overview-13deba?style=flat-square)](../README.md)

This is health data about women, and conclusions could shape how people think about risk. Every submission must address the points below.

| Rule | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Correlation is not causation** | Avoid causal language unless you can justify it. Say "associated with," not "causes." |
| **Synthetic versus real** | Dataset 2 is synthetic. Do not present findings from it as real-world clinical evidence. |
| **Ecological fallacy** | Do not use country-level results from Dataset 3 to make claims about individual women. |
| **Sex and gender** | These datasets record a binary variable. Note this limitation and avoid overgeneralizing to people outside the categories recorded. |
| **Not medical advice** | Nothing produced in this datathon is a diagnostic tool or clinical guidance. Include a short statement saying so in your final deliverables. |
| **Transparent uncertainty** | Report confidence intervals or variability where possible, and describe limitations. |
| **Data licenses** | Follow the terms on the original Kaggle pages and the article's CC BY-NC 4.0 license. Cite each source you use. The [source manifest](../data/provenance/source_manifest.md) lists the terms. |

## A Statement You Can Adapt

> This project is a student data analysis for the OpenBio 2026 Datathon. It is not medical advice, a diagnostic tool or clinical guidance. Findings from synthetic data are a methods exercise, and country-level findings do not describe individual people.
